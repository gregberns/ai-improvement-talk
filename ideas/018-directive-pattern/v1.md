## Why this is its own article

Article 017 is about the *mechanism* — two modes, how to trigger each, how to match mode to task. The directive pattern is adjacent but different: it's about *when to delegate a whole artifact* vs. *stay in the loop and iterate*.

The two concepts probably interact. Reasoning mode is often a collaborative, iterative interaction. Execution mode can be triggered as a one-shot directive. But they're not the same axis:

- **Mode** = what cognitive process the agent runs (think vs. do)
- **Pattern** = how the user engages (direct-and-delegate vs. iterate-and-refine)

Worth teasing apart in a separate article.

## The insight we want to land

Anthropic's Economic Index research (March 2026) found that experienced users are MORE iterative and LESS directive than newer users. That's the opposite of what Anthropic hypothesized — they expected experienced users to delegate more ("just do this whole thing, AI").

The implication for readers: if your instinct is "I'll just tell the agent to go build the feature," you're using a pattern that correlates with *less experience*, not more.

## What this article would cover

- Definition of directive vs. iterative in Anthropic's taxonomy — it's not just "commanding tone"
- The research finding and why it's counterintuitive

- How this relates to reasoning/execution modes (orthogonal? nested? same?)
