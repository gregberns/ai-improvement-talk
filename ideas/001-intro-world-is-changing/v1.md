# The World Is Changing and New Things Are Hard

**Category:** `[framing]`

## Core Idea

Empathetic opening. Acknowledge the struggle, then pivot to agency.

## The Setup

The job you loved — spending time refining and building beautiful systems — is changing underneath you. This is a long transition. Transitions are painful. Change is hard — can be fun, but disrupting fundamental beliefs is difficult and takes time.

We've been promised "These things are amazing" — but in practice, working with AI feels like working with a poorly performing mid-dev. It does a bunch of stuff, and more often than not makes a mess.

**Framing precision matters.** These engineers don't have the concerns media portrays:
- NOT "fear of AI replacing their job" — it's losing the job they LIKED
- NOT "AI not working well" — it's that AI behaves like a sloppy mid-dev
- NOT "lost" — it's a long, painful transition
- If we say "You're worried about X" and it's wrong, they'll tune out

## The Turn

What if it's not the tools that are sub-par — it's how we're using them?

(Image idea: pounding a screw in with a hammer)

You CAN work with staff-level AI engineers. They don't come out of the box. Without tuning, you won't see that level of performance. You need to learn how to evoke that from the models, agents, tools.

## The Promise

Structure: you have a symptom/pain point? Here's a possible solution to start experimenting with.

Don't limit yourself to spot fixes. Use these as a way to examine your "engineering process architecture." You are a system — understand how you, the system, works today, then use your engineering skills to improve those processes.

- Do we address the "I'm not using AI yet" crowd or assume they're already in?
